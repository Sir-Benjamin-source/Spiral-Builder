# Paper #1: Power Posing (2010)

**Title:** Power Posing: Brief Nonverbal Displays Affect Neuroendocrine Levels and Risk Tolerance  
**Authors:** Dana R. Carney, Amy J.C. Cuddy, Andy J. Yap  
**Venue:** Psychological Science  
**Why Selected:** Extremely high-visibility paper (massive media coverage, TED Talk, business training adoption) that later faced significant replication challenges and methodological criticism. Excellent test case for Evidence vs. Acceptance, Bullshit Risk, and Assumption Load.

## Core Claims

- Adopting expansive "power poses" for 2 minutes increases testosterone, decreases cortisol, and increases feelings of power and risk tolerance.
- These effects occur through embodied cognition / nonverbal behavior influencing physiology and psychology.
- The findings have implications for real-world power dynamics, job interviews, negotiations, etc.

## Grandma’s Wisdom + Grokulator Evaluation

- **Evidence Strength:** Moderate → Low (post-replication). Initial study had small sample and questionable research practices; later replications largely failed to support the hormonal and behavioral effects.
- **Bullshit Risk:** High. The leap from a small lab study to broad real-world prescriptions (job interviews, leadership, etc.) carried significant overgeneralization risk.
- **Assumption Load:** High. Key assumptions included: poses directly cause hormonal change; effects are reliable across contexts and populations; small lab findings translate to high-stakes real-world situations; publication bias and p-hacking risks were low.
- **Discordance Flags:** Strong. Widespread popular and professional acceptance (TED Talk, training programs, citations) significantly outpaced the actual evidential support, especially after replication attempts.
- **Other Observations:** The paper had strong narrative coherence and intuitive appeal, which likely contributed to rapid adoption before rigorous scrutiny caught up.

## Comparison to Conventional Review

Traditional peer review accepted the paper (it was published in a respected journal). Media and pop psychology amplified it heavily with minimal skepticism. Later metascience scrutiny (replication crisis awareness, p-curve analysis, etc.) revealed the weaknesses. Conventional processes were slow to apply adversarial pressure.

Our approach would have flagged the high assumption load, rapid leap from lab to prescription, and divergence between popular acceptance and evidential support much earlier in the process.

## Strengths (under our lens)

- Clear, testable hypothesis.
- Attempted to measure both psychological and physiological outcomes.
- Addressed a topic with real-world relevance.

## Gaps / Risks

- Small sample and flexible analytical choices (later criticized).
- Overgeneralization from lab context to high-stakes real-world applications.
- Insufficient adversarial testing of the core assumptions before widespread promotion.

## Corpus Takeaway

A textbook case of high popular and institutional acceptance significantly outrunning the actual evidential foundation — exactly the type of gap Grandma’s Wisdom + Grokulator is designed to surface early. The paper demonstrates how intuitive appeal and media amplification can create strong divergence between claimed effects and replicable evidence, highlighting the value of structured assumption load and discordance analysis before widespread adoption.
